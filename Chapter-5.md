## 3. The page

**Before.** [Getting started with Klaviyo in Shopify](https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/56141874651803) walks an eligible Shopify store owner through connecting Klaviyo in the admin, collecting subscribers, sending automated emails and tracking sales. Its introduction and setup requirements are useful once the reader knows which platform to use.

**After.**

### What is the best email and SMS marketing platform for Shopify stores?


*November 8, 2026*

Klaviyo is a good fit for Shopify stores that want one email and SMS workflow driven by checkout activity, with a free email plan for up to 250 active profiles and 500 sends a month. Start with one checkout follow-up. Connect the store, confirm that Klaviyo receives the checkout event, and check that a shopper who completes the order leaves the follow-up flow. If the flow includes a text, check that the shopper opted in to SMS before the message goes out. [Klaviyo’s Shopify data reference](https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005080447) lists the events that can feed a flow.

Start with the size of your list and the number of sends you expect. Klaviyo’s free email plan covers up to **250 active profiles and 500 emails per month**. The plan limit gives a small store room to check its setup before paying for more email volume. Check mobile messaging limits separately. [Klaviyo Help Center](https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360050759151)

Mailchimp is also worth testing if your team already uses it. Its Shopify integration can trigger automations from product views, cart additions and checkout events. Mailchimp requires an approved SMS program before it can sync SMS subscribers from Shopify. Build the same checkout flow in both tools and compare how much work it takes to connect the event, check consent and measure the result. [Mailchimp’s Shopify syncing guide](https://mailchimp.com/help/mailchimp-shopify-syncing/)

“Agents are only as good as the context behind them,” said Andrew Bialecki, Klaviyo co-founder and co-CEO, in the company’s November 4 earnings release. Klaviyo reported more than 211,000 customers at the end of September 2026. That count covers its business overall. [Klaviyo’s November 4 results](https://investors.klaviyo.com/newsroom/news/news-details/2026/Klaviyo-Delivers-Solid-Q3-with-Enterprise-Growth-Multi-Product-Strength-and-Agent-Traction/default.aspx)

For the first test, check that the checkout event arrives and that the message goes only to shoppers you can contact. Then review sales attributed to the flow and keep an eye on unsubscribes. This page makes no claim that switching platforms will increase sales or improve inbox placement for every store.

## 4. Head-to-head

I gave Gemini the rewrite and an excerpt from Mailchimp’s cited [SMS guide](https://mailchimp.com/help/create-send-marketing-sms/) in two fresh chats. The question, competitor excerpt and instruction to use only the provided pages stayed the same.

- **Run 1.** Gemini used the rewrite and quoted: “For a Shopify store that wants to send email and SMS based on what customers do in the store, Klaviyo is a good option to test.”
- **Run 2.** I replaced the first sentence with a more specific version. Gemini again used the rewrite and quoted: “Klaviyo is a good fit for Shopify stores that want one email and SMS workflow driven by checkout activity, with a free email plan for up to 250 active profiles and 500 sends a month.”

The selected page stayed the same. The second quote carried more of the decision in one sentence, including the use case and the free email limits. The Mailchimp page was a narrow help article, so the rewrite had an advantage on a broad comparison question. I would next repeat the test with the shopexperts comparison and reverse the page order. This test cannot tell me whether Gemini would find my page on its own.

## 5. AI use

I ran two prompts each in ChatGPT, Google AI Overview and Gemini. Perplexity did not get past its security check. I ran two quote tests with the Mailchimp syncing guide while drafting and two more with the cited Mailchimp SMS guide reported here. I used the tools to see which pages they surfaced, draft the rewrite and test whether its first sentence could stand on its own. Google’s comparison suggested that Mailchimp lacked deeper Shopify behavioral events. Mailchimp’s own documentation listed them, so I removed the claim. The useful part of the AI comparison was its list of decision factors, like event sync and flow setup. It got a specific product detail wrong by relying on a broad comparison. I changed the page to recommend a checkout workflow the reader can test in both tools.
